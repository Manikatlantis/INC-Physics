# Novelty Check & Literature Review: Temperature-Hole Extrapolation with a Temperature-Conditioned GFlowNet for the 2D Ising Model

## TL;DR
- **The core scientific idea — training a generative/amortized model only away from criticality and then inferring the phase transition (Tc, specific-heat divergence) in the unseen window — is NOT new in general.** It was done for the 2D Ising model with a diffusion-based "Thermodynamic Maps" model (Herron, Mondal, Schneekloth & Tiwary, PNAS 2024, trained on two temperatures asymmetrically spaced about Tc), and earlier for the XY model with a conditional GAN "interpolation trick" (Singh, Arora, Gupta & Scheurer, SciPost Phys. 11, 043, 2021). The honest novelty is method-specific, not conceptual.
- **The idea IS new for GFlowNets specifically.** No GFlowNet paper to date uses the trajectory-balance learned logZ to compute free energy/specific heat or to locate Tc for a spin system; EB-GFN (Zhang et al., ICML 2022) used the Ising energy only as a probabilistic-modeling benchmark (recovering couplings), with no temperature conditioning and no thermodynamics. The learned-logZ→specific-heat route is the sharpest defensible novelty — though even it is partly anticipated by variational-generative work that matched free energy "to the second-order derivative" (Li, Zhang & Pan, PRL 2024).
- **The frustrated triangular-lattice negative control is an established diagnostic** in supervised/unsupervised ML-for-phases work (used since Wang & Zhai 2017 / Hu, Singh & Scalettar 2017) but has NOT been done with an amortized neural sampler — so applying it as a "does-not-hallucinate-a-transition" control for a GFlowNet is genuinely novel.

## Key Findings

**Verdict 1 — Temperature-hole extrapolation to unseen criticality (any amortized/generative sampler): ALREADY DONE (not novel in general; novel only for GFlowNets).** The closest prior art, Herron et al. (PNAS 121, e2321971121, 2024, "Thermodynamic Maps"), trained a score-based/diffusion generative model on 32×32 2D Ising configurations at only two temperatures deep in the two phases (deliberately away from Tc≈2.269), then generated configurations across all intermediate temperatures and recovered the transition via a divergence in the heat capacity plus critical exponents. In the authors' words, they "demonstrate the ability of thermodynamic maps to infer the ferromagnetic phase transition of the Ising model, including temperature-dependent heat capacity and critical exponents, despite never having seen samples from the transition region." This is conceptually almost identical to the planned experiment, differing mainly in generative family (diffusion vs GFlowNet) and in using two point temperatures rather than a continuous excluded band.

**Verdict 2 — Locating Tc from a learned partition function / free-energy curvature (specific heat = second derivative of learned free energy): PARTIALLY DONE for other generative families; NOT done for GFlowNet logZ.** Wu, Wang & Zhang (VAN, PRL 122, 080602, 2019) computed the variational free energy and thermal observables including specific heat for the 2D Ising model, but trained a separate network per temperature. Li, Zhang & Pan (PRL 2024, "Deep Generative Modeling of the Canonical Ensemble with Differentiable Thermal Properties," arXiv:2404.18404) is the most direct match: they made temperature a differentiable input and showed the "differentiable free energy aligns closely with the exact one to the second-order derivative, indicating that the variational model captures the subtle thermal transitions at the phase transitions," validated on Ising and XY. No GFlowNet has done this with the trajectory-balance logZ.

**Verdict 3 — Frustrated triangular-lattice negative control: standard in ML-for-phases, novel for amortized samplers.** Hu, Singh & Scalettar (PRE 95, 062122, 2017) and Wang & Zhai (PRB 96, 144432, 2017) used the fully frustrated triangular-lattice antiferromagnetic Ising model as a test that ML methods pick out the correct physics. Wannier (1950) established that this model has residual ground-state entropy S₀/N ≈ 0.323 kB per spin and no finite-temperature phase transition — its specific heat is a single non-diverging dome. Using it as a "does the sampler hallucinate a transition?" control for a GFlowNet/amortized sampler has not been reported.

## Details

### Research Question 1 — Has the temperature-hole test been done with any amortized/generative sampler?

**Yes, the essential experiment exists for other generative families:**

1. **Herron, Mondal, Schneekloth & Tiwary, PNAS 121, e2321971121 (2024); arXiv:2308.14885.** Diffusion/score-based generative model ("Thermodynamic Maps," TM) on the 32×32 2D Ising model. Trained only at two temperatures deep in the two phases (ferromagnetic and paramagnetic, asymmetrically spaced about Tc≈2.269), then generated configurations at all temperatures and inferred the transition: a divergence in the heat capacity plus magnetization and specific-heat critical exponents, "despite never having seen samples from the transition region." This is the single most threatening piece of prior art for the planned experiment.

2. **Singh, Arora, Gupta & Scheurer, SciPost Phys. 11, 043 (2021); arXiv:2006.11868.** Conditional GAN / implicit generative model on the 2D XY model. They explicitly trained on temperatures outside the critical region (an "interpolation trick," e.g., excising a band around criticality) and interpolated into the unseen critical window: "the model can reliably generate samples in the vicinity of the phase transition, even when it has not been trained in the critical region," using a "GAN-fidelity measure that captures the model's susceptibility to external changes of parameters" as an unsupervised transition indicator. This is a temperature-hole experiment with a generative sampler, on XY rather than Ising.

3. **Lee, Wang, Herron & Tiwary, "Exponentially Tilted Thermodynamic Maps (expTM)," arXiv:2503.15080 (2025).** Extends TM to lattice-gas (grand canonical) and CO2 (isothermal-isobaric); the framework characterizes "generic attributes of phase transitions from very limited observations made deep within different phases' domains of stability," now across temperature, pressure, and chemical potential.

4. **Boyda et al., "Machine-learning physics from unphysics: Finding deconfinement temperature… from outside the scaling window," Phys. Rev. D 103, 014509 (2021); arXiv:2009.10971.** Not generative, but the same philosophy: a network trained at unphysical couplings predicts the deconfinement order parameter in the physical region — "a numerical analog of the analytical continuation from easily accessible but physically uninteresting regions of the coupling space to the interesting but potentially not accessible regions." Establishes "predict criticality from a region you didn't train in" as a recognized paradigm.

5. **Temperature-transfer generative samplers (adjacent, no explicit criticality-hole test):** TRADE (Wahl et al., AISTATS 2025, arXiv:2410.19492), Temperature-Steerable Flows (Dibak et al., PRR 4, L042005, 2022; arXiv:2012.00429), Temperature-Annealed Boltzmann Generators (ICML 2025, arXiv:2501.19077). These learn temperature-conditioned Boltzmann distributions and can extrapolate in T, but focus on molecular sampling, not on detecting a phase transition in a held-out window.

**Implication:** The high-level claim "can an amortized neural sampler predict a phase transition it never saw during training?" is essentially answered "yes" in the literature for diffusion (Ising) and GAN (XY). The GFlowNet-specific instantiation is unoccupied.

### Research Question 2 — How established is "detect a transition without training near/at it" in supervised ML-for-phases?

Very well established; this is not novel in general.
- **Carrasquilla & Melko, Nature Physics 13, 431 (2017):** supervised CNN/FC classifiers trained on configurations away from Tc; the transition is located where the softmax output crosses 0.5.
- **van Nieuwenburg, Liu & Huber, Nature Physics 13, 435 (2017):** "learning by confusion" locates transitions without labels via the W-shaped accuracy curve; demonstrated on the classical Ising model.
- **Xu, Fu & Zhao, arXiv:1901.00774 (2019):** explicitly train "with samples only obtained at a non-critical parameter point" and still locate the critical point — the supervised analog of the planned experiment.
- Related canon: Wang (unsupervised PCA, PRB 94, 195105, 2016); Wetzel (autoencoders, PRE 96, 022140, 2017); Ch'ng et al. (PRX 7, 031038, 2017).

So for classifiers/unsupervised methods, "don't train at criticality, still find it" is a decade-old, mature result. The planned work's novelty cannot rest on the concept; it must rest on doing it with a generative amortized sampler whose learned normalizing constant (logZ) yields thermodynamics.

### Research Question 3 — Learned partition function / free energy → Tc via specific-heat curvature

- **Wu, Wang & Zhang, "Solving Statistical Mechanics Using Variational Autoregressive Networks," PRL 122, 080602 (2019); arXiv:1809.10606.** VAN computes variational free energy (an upper bound, F[q]=−(1/β)lnZ), plus entropy, magnetization, correlations, and specific heat, for the 2D Ising, Hopfield, and SK models. However, VAN trains a separate model at each β/temperature; it does not produce logZ(T) as a single differentiable function and locate Tc from its curvature in one shot. This is the closest VAN prior art and a must-cite.
- **Li, Zhang & Pan, PRL (2024), arXiv:2404.18404:** the decisive prior art for the learned-logZ route. A temperature-differentiable variational model gives thermodynamic quantities as continuous functions of temperature, with the "differentiable free energy align[ing] closely with the exact one to the second-order derivative," capturing Ising/XY transitions. This is exactly the "derivative curvature of the learned free energy locates the transition" idea, though with autoregressive/flow density models rather than a GFlowNet.
- **GFlowNet side:** GFlowNet Foundations (arXiv:2111.09266) and Trajectory Balance (Malkin et al., arXiv:2201.13259) note that GFlowNets learn Z explicitly (the logZ scalar) and can in principle estimate partition functions/free energies — but as a stated capability, never operationalized to compute specific heat or Tc for a spin system. No GFlowNet paper found does this.

**Implication:** Extracting specific heat from the trajectory-balance logZ(T) of a temperature-conditioned GFlowNet and locating Tc from its peak is novel for GFlowNets, but the general technique (differentiate a learned free-energy curve twice to see the transition) is already published for other generative models. The novelty is "first to do it with a GFlowNet's logZ," not "first to do it."

### Research Question 4 — GFlowNets / amortized samplers on triangular-lattice / frustrated Ising, and frustrated systems as negative controls

- **No GFlowNet or amortized-sampler study of the triangular-lattice (frustrated or ferromagnetic) Ising model was found.** GFlowNet spin-system work is limited to EB-GFN's square-Ising probabilistic-modeling benchmark (recovering the coupling matrix J from samples; no temperature, no thermodynamics).
- **Frustrated triangular-lattice AFM Ising as a negative/ML-stress-test control is standard in the non-generative literature:** Hu, Singh & Scalettar, PRE 95, 062122 (2017) used PCA on the triangular-lattice Ising antiferromagnet (power-law correlations at T=0, no finite-T transition) to test whether ML picks out incipient order; Wang & Zhai (PRB 96, 144432, 2017; Frontiers of Physics 13, 130507, 2018) "Machine learning of frustrated classical spin models" used PCA/kernel-PCA; Acevedo, Arlego & Lamas (PRB 103, 134422, 2021) and Corte et al. (Comput. Mater. Sci. 198, 110702, 2021) used confusion/NN methods on frustrated square-lattice J1–J2 models.
- **The physics is textbook and exactly known:** the nearest-neighbor triangular-lattice antiferromagnet is fully frustrated with a macroscopically degenerate ground state (Wannier's exact residual entropy S₀/N ≈ 0.323 kB per spin) and no ordering at any finite temperature — its specific heat is a single non-diverging dome. The ferromagnetic triangular lattice has exact Tc = 4/ln 3 ≈ 3.641 (in units J/kB).

**Implication:** Running the frustrated triangular AFM as a "does the amortized sampler hallucinate a spurious specific-heat peak?" control, and the ferromagnetic triangular lattice as a "finds a different known Tc" positive control, is novel specifically for amortized/GFlowNet samplers. The negative-control concept itself is borrowed from established supervised/unsupervised practice, which should be cited.

## Recommendations

**Staged next steps:**

1. **Reframe the novelty claim now, before running experiments.** Do not claim "first to show an amortized sampler can predict an unseen transition" — Herron et al. (2024) and Singh et al. (2021) block that. Instead claim the GFlowNet-specific and mechanism-specific contribution. Recommended framing sentence:
   > "We present the first temperature-conditioned GFlowNet that recovers the 2D Ising critical temperature from the curvature of its trajectory-balance–learned log-partition function, and we show it remains predictive when trained with a temperature hole excising the critical region — the first amortized-sampler demonstration that uses the model's own learned normalizing constant (rather than sample observables alone) to locate Tc, validated against frustrated (no-transition) and ferromagnetic (shifted-Tc) triangular-lattice controls."

2. **Lead with the logZ route as the primary novelty, sample-observables as corroboration.** The learned-logZ→specific-heat route is where GFlowNets are genuinely differentiated (they carry an explicit logZ scalar, unlike GANs/diffusion). The magnetization/susceptibility/Binder-cumulant route largely reproduces what Herron/Singh already showed, so present it as a cross-check, not the headline.

3. **Run the temperature-hole experiment as a direct, harder analog of Herron et al.** Their two-point training is arguably a bigger extrapolation than your two-band hole; to claim added value, (a) quantify how hole width degrades Tc recovery with a systematic sweep (which they did not do), and (b) compare the logZ-derived Cv peak against the sample-observable estimate within the same model.

4. **Execute the triangular-lattice controls — this is your cleanest untouched ground.** No amortized sampler has been tested for "phantom transition" behavior on a fully frustrated system. A convincing negative result (flat/non-diverging Cv from logZ on the triangular AFM, consistent with Wannier's single-dome specific heat) plus a positive result (Tc≈3.641 on the ferromagnetic triangular lattice) would be a distinctive, defensible contribution.

5. **Must-cite baseline list** (the write-up will be weak before referees if these are missing):
   - GFlowNet core: Bengio et al. GFlowNet Foundations (arXiv:2111.09266); Malkin et al. Trajectory Balance (arXiv:2201.13259); Zhang et al. EB-GFN (arXiv:2202.01361).
   - Generative-sampler prior art on the exact idea: Herron et al. PNAS 2024 (arXiv:2308.14885); Singh, Arora, Gupta, Scheurer, SciPost Phys. 11, 043 (2021) (arXiv:2006.11868); Li, Zhang, Pan PRL 2024 (arXiv:2404.18404); Lee et al. expTM 2025 (arXiv:2503.15080).
   - Free-energy/VAN: Wu, Wang, Zhang PRL 2019 (arXiv:1809.10606).
   - Supervised/unsupervised phase-ML canon: Carrasquilla & Melko 2017; van Nieuwenburg, Liu, Huber 2017; Wang 2016; Hu, Singh, Scalettar 2017; Wang & Zhai 2017.
   - "Outside the scaling window" paradigm: Boyda et al. PRD 2021 (arXiv:2009.10971).

**Benchmarks that would change the recommendation:** If a targeted search of 2025–2026 GFlowNet-for-physics preprints turns up a temperature-conditioned GFlowNet applied to spin thermodynamics or Tc, the "first for GFlowNets" claim collapses to "first with a temperature hole + frustrated control," and the paper should pivot entirely to the systematic hole-width study and the negative control. Conversely, if the logZ-derived Cv proves quantitatively more accurate than sample-observable estimates, elevate that to the central result.

## Caveats
- **Negative-existence claims are search-limited.** The conclusion that no GFlowNet has computed spin-system Tc from logZ is based on thorough but not exhaustive search; the GFlowNet-for-physics literature is moving fast, and a 2025–2026 preprint could exist. State this honestly in the paper.
- **"Closest prior art" is a matter of framing.** Herron et al. (diffusion) and Li/Zhang/Pan (temperature-differentiable free energy) each individually cover a large part of the planned contribution; a skeptical referee could argue the GFlowNet is merely a new backbone for an established idea. The defense must be that (a) the GFlowNet's explicit logZ is a qualitatively different and more direct route to free energy than sample-fluctuation estimates, and (b) the systematic hole-width degradation study and frustrated negative control are genuinely new.
- **Finite-size effects dominate small-lattice exponents.** Herron et al. reported specific-heat exponents far from ideal values due to finite size; any critical-exponent claims from GFlowNet samples on modest lattices should be presented with matched MCMC baselines and finite-size scaling, not as absolute values.
- **VAN / Li-Zhang-Pan use variational free energy (an upper bound).** The trajectory-balance logZ is also only approximate; validate logZ(T) against the exact Onsager free energy (square lattice) before trusting its second derivative near Tc, where neural samplers are known to be least accurate (mode-mixing/critical slowing down).
- **The two closest works differ in generative family, which is the crux of the novelty.** The claim's durability depends entirely on GFlowNets being treated by the community as a distinct enough method; if reviewers view "amortized generative sampler" as one category, the contribution reads as incremental. Positioning around the explicit-logZ mechanism and the untouched frustrated-control experiment is the strongest hedge.
